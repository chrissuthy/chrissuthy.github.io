Research

My primary research interest is spatial population ecology, with a specific focus on characterising how space use, dispersal, and demography vary in response to changes in density, the environment, and landscape structure. The overarching theme of my research is an emphasis on increasing the biological realism of modern eco-statistical models so we can best test spatial ecological theory.

Teaching

At the University of St Andrews I teach two courses in the Statistical Ecology MSc programme:

  • Modelling Wildlife Population Dynamics (MT5767)
  • Estimating Animal Abundance and Biodiversity (MT5751)

Previously at the University of Massachusetts–Amherst:

  • Introduction to Quantitative Ecology (NRC 290b)
  • Applied Ecological Statistics (ECO 636)
  • Programming in R (Honors college seminar)
  • Tools for Science Communication (ECO 691)

MSc Programme

I coordinate the Statistical Ecology MSc at St Andrews — a one-year taught degree where students learn the modern statistical methods used by professionals in ecology.
